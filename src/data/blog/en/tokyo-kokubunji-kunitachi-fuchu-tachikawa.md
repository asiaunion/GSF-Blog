In [Ep.09](/posts/tokyo-musashino-mitaka-chofu/), we covered the western premium suburbs of Musashino, Mitaka, and Chofu. This episode moves further west along the Chuo Line into the **Tama Education & Culture Belt**: **Kokubunji City, Kunitachi City, Fuchu City, and Tachikawa City**.

---

## First, the bottom line

- The four Tama cities are **not a single "cheap suburb"**—they are **four distinct markets** differentiated by rail lines, redevelopment activity, and land use.
- **Even within the same city**, prices per ㎡ diverge by **more than 3x** depending on the cho-me (e.g., Tachikawa: Akebono-cho 83.4万 vs. Ichibang-cho 23.0万).
- **Despite wide purchase-price spreads**, new-build 1R rents near stations converge at **8.0–9.4万円/月**. Low-price cho-me offer relatively wider gross yields; redevelopment cho-me carry a price premium.
- **Population and income** provide background context. In this dataset, the axis that more directly predicts purchase prices is **station access, rail line, and cho-me redevelopment potential** rather than city-level income.
- All four cities carry **flood and liquefaction risk flags**. When evaluating individual cho-me, cross-reference the relevant hazard maps separately.

---

## Why this article

The reading of "Tama = cheaper suburbs beyond the 23 wards" is common. Kunitachi as a university town, Tachikawa as a regional hub, Fuchu with its government offices and racecourse—these are often lumped together as a single "value" corridor.

This time, we tested that assumption using **cho-me-level transaction prices** rather than city averages. Price gaps that city names alone cannot explain kept appearing, which led us to reframe the analysis around **living zones, rail lines, and redevelopment** axes.

My starting hypothesis was **"Tama = discounted, value-oriented suburbs."** When I compared the cho-me data point by point, that hypothesis failed repeatedly.

---

**Starting hypothesis:** Areas beyond the 23 wards in Tama are broadly discounted, and the four cities sit in a similar "suburban premium" tier.

**What the data changed:**

| Trigger | Observation |

## 1. Four Cities, Four Markets

The cho-me tables below are designed to show **which areas within a city command a premium**—not which city is cheapest overall. City-wide averages can dilute locally high-price cho-me.

### Kokubunji City — Western Hub and Honcho Premium

Kokubunji Station records approximately **30.6万人/日**, making it the clearest hub among the four cities. City-wide ㎡ price rose from 58.7万円 in 2021 to **82.2万円 in 2025** (4-year CAGR **8.8%**). 70㎡ ≈ **5,754万円** (approximately **42.2%** of Chiyoda Ward [Ep.01] benchmark).

| Cho-me | Price/㎡ (transaction) | 70㎡ estimate | Count |
|--------|----------------------|---------------|-------|
| Honcho | 126.5万円 | approx. 8,855万円 | 37 |
| Higashi Koigakubo | 70.6万円 | approx. 4,942万円 | 46 |
| Nishi-cho | 45.4万円 | approx. 3,178万円 | 16 |
| **City average** | **82.2万円** | **approx. 5,754万円** | **156** |

**Key observation:** Honcho's 126.5万円 is the highest cho-me figure across all four cities. Public records link this to **Kokubunji Station North Exit redevelopment** (City Tower Kokubunji, etc.) and the JR Chuo Line / Seibu Line interchange. This aligns directionally with Joseph's hypothesis on "western hub rail connectivity and large-scale redevelopment." However, with n=37 and cho-me aggregation, we avoid asserting specific walk-distance conclusions.

### Kunitachi City — University Town, Cho-me Dispersion

Home to Hitotsubashi University, Tokyo Gakugei University, and other **academic and residential institutions**. Kunitachi Station records approximately **9.4万人/日**—smaller than the hubs of Kokubunji and Tachikawa. ㎡ price: 53.8万 → **64.2万** (CAGR **4.5%**), 70㎡ ≈ **4,494万円** (approximately **33.0%** of Chiyoda).

| Cho-me | Price/㎡ (transaction) | 70㎡ estimate | Count |
|--------|----------------------|---------------|-------|
| Higashi | 73.4万円 | approx. 5,138万円 | 23 |
| Naka | 70.4万円 | approx. 4,928万円 | 22 |
| Fujimidai | 58.0万円 | approx. 4,060万円 | 28 |
| Aoyagi | 44.3万円 | approx. 3,101万円 | 7 |
| **City average** | **64.2万円** | **approx. 4,494万円** | **105** |

**Where the hypothesis broke down:** Per-capita income ranks **#1 among the four cities (191.9万)**, yet the highest-price cho-me is lower than Kokubunji Honcho. The framing of "university town = uniform premium" does not hold—cho-me-level differentiation (Higashi/Naka vs. Aoyagi/Yagawa) is more accurate. Station traffic and large-scale redevelopment catalysts are comparatively weak here.

### Fuchu City — Government/Racecourse, Miyanishi-cho and Periphery

The **administrative and entertainment axis** anchored by Tokyo Racecourse and Fuchu Station. Bubaigawara Station records approximately **15.7万人/日**; the city has 14 stations. ㎡ price: 48.9万 → **60.3万** (CAGR **5.4%**), 70㎡ ≈ **4,221万円** (approximately **31.0%** of Chiyoda).

| Cho-me | Price/㎡ (transaction) | 70㎡ estimate | Count |
|--------|----------------------|---------------|-------|
| Miyanishi-cho | 89.6万円 | approx. 6,272万円 | 45 |
| Fuchu-cho | 75.3万円 | approx. 5,271万円 | 24 |
| Shiraito-dai | 50.0万円 | approx. 3,500万円 | 35 |
| Yotsuya | 34.9万円 | approx. 2,443万円 | 18 |
| **City average** | **60.3万円** | **approx. 4,221万円** | **370** |

**Where interpretation diverges:** The contrast between **Miyanishi-cho** and **Shiraito-dai** is pronounced. Transaction volume (370 cases) is the highest among the four cities, indicating good **market liquidity**. The data reads as a dual structure: cho-me premium tied to the administrative/entertainment context vs. peripheral low-price cho-me.

### Tachikawa City — Low City Average, High Intra-City Dispersion

A city with a strong **administrative and commercial sub-center** image. Tachikawa Station records approximately **30.1万人/日**; combined city-station traffic approximately **52.6万人/日**. ㎡ price: 42.1万 → **50.8万** (CAGR **4.8%**), 70㎡ ≈ **3,556万円** — **lowest city average among the four** (approximately **26.1%** of Chiyoda).

| Cho-me | Price/㎡ (transaction) | 70㎡ estimate | Count |
|--------|----------------------|---------------|-------|
| Akebono-cho | 83.4万円 | approx. 5,838万円 | 34 |
| Nishiki-cho | 57.4万円 | approx. 4,018万円 | 57 |
| Ichiban-cho | 23.0万円 | approx. 1,610万円 | 19 |
| **City average** | **50.8万円** | **approx. 3,556万円** | **209** |

**Where thinking shifted:** Looking at only the city average suggests "Tama's lowest-price market," but **Akebono-cho** is in the 80万 range. Joseph's T1 observation on "infrastructure quality, scale of redevelopment" aligns directionally with the Akebono-cho vs. Ichiban-cho contrast and public redevelopment data (Tachikawa Takurosu, etc.). Note that Ichiban-cho n=19 — treat as directional reference only.

## 2. The Split within Tachikawa

Grouping everything under "Tachikawa" obscures the following gap:

**Akebono-cho** — JR Tachikawa and Tama Monorail Tachikawa-Kita interchange zone. Since the Tachikawa Takurosu North Exit redevelopment (opened 2016), this has become a commercial/new-build mansion corridor. Station-proximity premiums are consistent across public land price and sales data.

**Ichiban-cho** — Closer to the Seibu Tachikawa living zone, with a high share of **Class 1 Exclusively Low-Rise Residential** zoning. A ㎡ price in the 20万 range signals not "Tachikawa is cheap" but rather **a different network and low-rise land-use classification**.

Joseph's T1 intuition (Akebono-cho has better infrastructure; the redevelopment scale difference) is **directionally consistent with public L1 data**. For safety, the text uses **MLIT cho-me labels** rather than specific "X-minute walk" assertions.

## 3. Purchase Prices and Rents: Two Different Maps

This table is designed to examine **whether rents track purchase prices**. City-average ㎡ prices and SUUMO 1R rents (new build, 1–5 min walk) are placed side by side.

| City | SUUMO 1R (万円/月) | City avg. ㎡ price | Gross yield* |
|------|-----------------------|-------------------|-------------|
| Kokubunji | 9.4 | 82.2万円 | ~1.9% |
| Kunitachi | 9.3 | 64.2万円 | ~2.5% |
| Fuchu | 8.6 | 60.3万円 | ~2.4% |
| Tachikawa | 8.0 | 50.8万円 | ~2.7% |

\*70㎡, city-avg ㎡ price, pre-tax, excluding management fees and vacancy. Structural comparison only — not investment advice.

**At the cho-me level:** A low-purchase-price cho-me like Tachikawa Ichiban-cho (23.0万/㎡) could show a gross yield of **4–6%** assuming the same city-level 1R rent (8.0万円/月) — though actual Ichiban-cho 1R rents may be lower. **Kokubunji Honcho** (126.5万円) and **Tachikawa Akebono-cho** (83.4万) carry large purchase premiums, compressing gross yield to approximately **~1.3–1.6%**.

**Recommended reading order:** Cho-me → rail line → redevelopment → then rent and yield. The purchase-price map and the rent map are not the same.

## 6. The Counter-Hypothesis and Its Limits

**Counter-hypothesis:** "Tama is discounted versus the 23 wards, so city averages are sufficient."

This reading holds when **city-level beta** reasserts itself—e.g., during rate declines or a broad suburban rally. In the current dataset, city averages mask locally high-price cho-me like Honcho, Akebono-cho, and Miyanishi-cho, and erase the 3.6x intra-Tachikawa gap.

**Another counter-hypothesis:** "High-income Kunitachi is therefore undervalued and a rising candidate."

From 2021–25, Kunitachi CAGR (4.5%) lagged Kokubunji (8.8%), and the #1 income city and the #1 highest-price cho-me do not coincide. The income-first framework for city selection is weak in this dataset.

## 7. Who This Fits / Who It Doesn't

### Who this may suit

- Those who want to drill down to **cho-me, rail line, and redevelopment** before selecting a location
- Those who want to **read purchase prices and rent/yield separately**
- Those with a budget that falls outside the 23 wards but want to explore the **western hub and Tama living zone**
- Those who want to understand **the four-market difference** first, from a long-term residency or owner-occupier perspective

### Who this may not suit

- Those who look at **only the city name** and conclude "Tama = cheap"
- Those who map **income rank = price rank** in a simple one-to-one correspondence
- Those who want to judge purely from city averages **without checking disaster risk** (flood/liquefaction)
- Those targeting only short-term capital gains by riding **city-level beta**

## 8. Joseph's View

> A synthesis of **post-data judgments** based on the Decision Log and back-and-forth analysis. Field anecdotes and fictional experiences are not included.

**The thinking most revised by this data**  
The "Tama = cheap suburbs" assumption breaks against **Kokubunji Honcho's 126.5万円** and the **3.6x intra-Tachikawa gap**. **Cho-me and living zone** take priority over city average.

**The judgment I will continue to hold**  
Read A, B, C in parallel — four-market separation, Tachikawa's internal dual structure, and the dual reading of purchase vs. rent. **Purchase-price map ≠ rent map.**

**What remains hard to be certain about**  
Cho-me with n<30 (Aoyagi 7 cases, Ichiban-cho 19 cases)—treat **direction only**. Public L1 cross-validation of T1/T2 intuitions (T3) confirmed by Joseph (agreed).

**What readers should verify first**  
① MLIT transaction distribution for the **cho-me** of interest ② Public data on **rail lines and redevelopment** ③ Location-specific **hazard maps** ④ Actual **rent listings** on SUUMO etc. (gross yield is structural comparison only).

**In one line** — The Tama Education & Culture Belt is not one cheap neighborhood but **four markets**, and even within the same city, **cho-me, rail line, and redevelopment diverge**. Purchase prices vary widely while new-build station-adjacent rents converge in the **8–9万円/月帯**, so the right sequence is: **choose location first**, then examine rent and yield.
