In Japanese real estate investment, the exit (sale) phase is just as critical as the acquisition and rental management stages. For non-resident investors residing in Korea, the tax obligations and settlement processes that occur during a sale operate in a highly unique manner.

Failing to understand the tax laws and transaction flows at the exit stage can lead to a significant drop in actual net proceeds, even if the pre-tax capital gain appears substantial. Before signing a sales contract, it is essential to map out the capital gains tax requirements in both Japan and Korea.

---

## 1. Japanese Capital Gains Tax: The Trap of the 'January 1st' Rule

Under Japanese tax law, capital gains derived from the sale of real property by an individual are taxed separately from other income sources under the Separate Classification Taxation (分離課税) system. The primary variable in determining the tax rate is the holding period.

Whether the holding period is classified as short-term or long-term dramatically affects the tax rate. However, simply calculating the number of calendar days from the acquisition date to the sale date can lead to costly errors.

### The 'January 1st of the Sale Year' Standard
According to the National Tax Agency (NTA) of Japan, the holding period is determined based on the status as of **January 1st of the year in which the property is sold**, not the actual date of the sale itself. <sup><a href="#source-1" id="cite-1">1</a></sup>

For example, if you acquired a Tokyo condo on May 1, 2021, and sold it on June 1, 2026, the calendar holding period is 5 years and 1 month. However, for tax purposes, the holding period is evaluated as of January 1, 2026, which is only 4 years and 8 months. Consequently, this transaction is classified as a **Short-term Capital Gain** and is subject to the higher tax rate.

### Applicable Tax Rates for Non-Residents
For non-resident individual investors, the tax rates on real estate capital gains are structured as follows. Since non-residents do not maintain local registration, they are generally exempt from local inhabitant tax (住民税), and only national income tax (including the Special Reconstruction Income Tax) applies. <sup><a href="#source-2" id="cite-2">2</a></sup>

- **Short-term Capital Gains (Holding period of 5 years or less)**: Income Tax Rate of 30.63%
- **Long-term Capital Gains (Holding period exceeding 5 years)**: Income Tax Rate of 15.315%

Because a difference of just a few months in the transaction calendar can double the tax burden, you should confirm that the January 1st criteria for the 5-year threshold is met before finalizing the closing date.

## 2. Withholding Tax at Sale and the Tax Return Settlement Flow

![Japan Property Capital Gains Tax Flow Diagram](/assets/images/blog/diagrams/korea-resident-japan-property-capital-gains-tax-flow.webp)

To prevent tax evasion by non-residents who might repatriate proceeds immediately after a sale, Japanese tax law enforces a withholding tax mechanism on property transactions.

### 10.21% Withholding Tax on Gross Sales Proceeds
In principle, when a buyer pays the purchase price to a non-resident seller, the buyer is required to withhold **10.21% of the gross sale proceeds** (not the net gain) and remit it to the Japanese tax office. <sup><a href="#source-3" id="cite-3">3</a></sup> This withholding requirement on the gross amount can temporarily lock up a substantial portion of the seller's cash flow on the closing day.

### Withholding Tax Exemption Criteria
However, the withholding tax requirement is waived if the transaction meets all three of the following conditions: <sup><a href="#source-3" id="cite-3">3</a></sup>

1. The buyer is an **individual** (not a corporation).
2. The buyer or their relatives will use the property for **their own residence**.
3. The total sale price is **100,000,000 JPY or less**.

If you sell an investment property to a corporate buyer or to another non-resident investor, the 10.21% withholding tax will apply. It is recommended to factor this temporary reduction in cash proceeds into your exit plan.

### Tax Return Filing via a Tax Representative
The withheld amount is not the final tax liability. The non-resident seller must file a Japanese income tax return (確定申告) between February 16 and March 15 of the year following the sale.

By accounting for the actual acquisition cost, capital improvements, sales commissions, and accumulated depreciation, the final tax liability is calculated. If the actual tax owed is lower than the 10.21% withheld, the difference will be refunded. Since non-residents do not reside in Japan, they must appoint a local **Tax Representative (納税管理人)** to manage the filing and receive the tax refund.

## 3. Key Differences for Corporate Ownership

If you hold the property under a corporate entity rather than as an individual, the tax structure at the exit phase changes entirely.

For corporate entities, there is no distinction between short-term and long-term holding periods. Capital gains are added to the corporation's ordinary business profits and taxable income, and taxed at the prevailing corporate tax rates. If your exit strategy involves short-term turnover or managing multiple assets, corporate vs. individual ownership is a critical structural decision. ## 4. Korean Tax Obligations and the Foreign Tax Credit

Under South Korean tax law, residents are obligated to report and pay capital gains tax to the National Tax Service (NTS) when disposing of foreign real estate. <sup><a href="#source-4" id="cite-4">4</a></sup> This represents the cross-border tax segment where both Japanese and Korean tax codes apply.

### Reporting and Payment Procedures in Korea
- **Preliminary Return**: The taxpayer needs to file a preliminary capital gains tax return with the local tax office within 2 months from the end of the month in which the transfer date falls.
- **Final Return**: A final consolidated return must be filed in May of the following year.

The tax rate applied to foreign property capital gains in Korea follows the standard progressive tax rates.

### Eliminating Double Taxation: The Foreign Tax Credit
To prevent double taxation on the same gain in both countries, Korean tax law provides a **Foreign Tax Credit (외국납부세액공제)**. <sup><a href="#source-5" id="cite-5">5</a></sup> Under this system, the capital gains tax paid to the Japanese tax office can be credited against the Korean tax liability.

> [!WARNING]
> **Understanding Foreign Tax Credit Limits**
> The credit is limited to the portion of the Korean tax liability that corresponds to the foreign-source income. If the tax paid in Japan exceeds the Korean tax liability on the same income, or if certain expense deductions differ, the excess foreign tax may not be fully credited, resulting in residual double taxation.

## 5. Pre-Sale Tax & Administrative Checklist

A step-by-step checklist for managing your tax and administrative obligations prior to selling Japanese real estate:

### [Phase A] Contract & Closing
- [ ] **Verify Holding Period**: Confirm that the holding period exceeds 5 years as of January 1st of the sale year (to secure the long-term tax rate).
- [ ] **Evaluate Withholding Status**: Review the buyer's profile and use of the property to determine if the 10.21% withholding tax applies.
- [ ] **Estimate Net Proceeds**: Simulate net cash proceeds after accounting for Japanese withholding tax, transaction costs, depreciation recapture, and Korean tax liabilities. Use the [Rental Yield vs. Capital Gain Breakeven Guide](/en/posts/tokyo-small-rental-yield-vs-capital-gain-breakeven/) to assess your net baseline.

### [Phase B] Japanese Tax Reporting
- [ ] **Appoint Tax Representative**: Line up a local tax representative (typically a Japanese tax accountant) to manage the NTA filing and handle the refund of withheld taxes.
- [ ] **File Japanese Tax Return**: File the final tax return in Japan between February and March of the year following the sale to reconcile the actual tax liability.

### [Phase C] Korean Tax Reporting & Remittance
- [ ] **Prepare Documentation for Funds Transfer**: Gather copies of the sales contract, closing statements, and Japanese tax receipts to justify the bank remittance of sales proceeds to Korea.
- [ ] **File Korean Preliminary & Final Returns**: File the preliminary return within 2 months of the end of the sale month, and compile the final return in May of the following year.
- [ ] **Claim Foreign Tax Credit**: Attach the Japanese tax payment receipt to claim the foreign tax credit on your Korean tax return.

## 6. Investor Action

Immediate steps for investors looking to optimize a cross-border real estate exit:

- **Check Your Calendar**: If you are close to the 5-year threshold, consult your tax representative to adjust the closing date so that it crosses the January 1st mark of the next tax year.
- **Maintain Liquidity**: If the 10.21% withholding tax applies, ensure you have sufficient liquidity to cover transaction costs and debt payoff at closing, as the withholding refund may take several months.
- **Consolidate Receipts**: Keep PDF records of all acquisition invoices, capital improvement receipts, and Japanese tax return filings. These are required by both Korean tax authorities and banks handling the repatriation of funds.
